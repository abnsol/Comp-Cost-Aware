PRELIMINARY BASELINE CRITERIA REVIEW
2026-10-04. Review of existing implementation and results; no new native runs.

Decision
The planned preliminary measurement phase is complete. Retain this KB family and
its Python-led native PLN harness as a controlled development baseline for
first-valid-proof completion. It is not yet a complete research benchmark, and
the evidence does not establish that adding cost information improves answers.
Proceed to the agreed paper review, not directly to estimator implementation.

This decision does not silently discard the user's probabilistic-accuracy goal.
The later, explicitly agreed qualification contract in 01-kb-specification.txt
narrowed THIS phase to a valid derivation and real computation costs. Original
checklist requirements concerning probability accuracy, learned policies and
held-out comparisons remain visible below; they are not retroactive requirements
to treat an otherwise valid first-proof measurement as incorrect.

Evidence established
- Actual pinned PLN execution, driven by Python, with native selection/rules and
  queue transitions validated. This is the PeTTa/SWI subprocess path; execution
  via hyperon.MeTTa() or an embedded PeTTa class is not established.
- Nine constructed KBs have valid reachable answers. The first goal is reached
  after 4-6 expansions, with strength1 and confidence approximately0.729, and its
  actual ancestor proof is replayed against the pinned formulas/evidence rules.
- Eight of13 saved states passed the predeclared local expansion-cost criteria.
  All five width16 states passed, with paired B/A medians3.535-3.799. One width1
  control fell within the relative band; four states remain overhead-unresolved.
- Actual deadline sensitivity:630 budgeted runs,370 verified successes,260 misses.
  At0.25 ms:0/90 successes; at8 ms and16 ms:90/90 per budget. No late answer was
  credited. These are observations on fixed development cases, not new domains.
- Online goal/budget checks are charged to loaded-engine CPU. Cooperative stopping
  is explicit, with maximum observed overshoot1.890501 ms. Setup, serialization,
  parent orchestration and offline proof replay are outside this CPU interval.

The missing causal link
Local operation costs differ, and deadlines affect completion. Neither observation
establishes that selecting a cheaper pending item improves total query completion.
No factor-off/on policy comparison has run. In particular:
  * Native pending work includes implication rule records, not only ReadyA/B.
  * All first goals were generated while selecting a Certificate -> CanOperate
    rule. Canonical/swapped runs expand BOTH Ready facts before the first goal.
  * Shuffled width4 reaches a B proof at expansion6 before selecting ReadyB at7.
    Shuffled width16 reaches a B proof at5 before selecting ReadyA at23.
  * More B results can have downstream value. An expansion's CPU does not measure
    the remaining cost of a complete proof or its expected usefulness.
  * Swapping two equal-priority facts is not an isolated cost-information ablation.
    It preserves neither a cost-based decision rule nor proof of end-to-end gain.
These are reasons to keep all native candidates in a future comparison, not to
remove rule records so the two-route story becomes easier to demonstrate.

Original 18-criterion checklist
Statuses refer to each COMPLETE original criterion, with narrow achievements
and deferred parts stated separately. They are not a numerical project score.
MET is scoped to the observed implementation/tasks; UNMET means required work
has not been supplied; UNRESOLVED means evidence/design is insufficient to decide.

1. Precise incremental speed/accuracy question -- UNRESOLVED in full.
   First-proof time and deadline success are now explicit. A matched cost-factor
   intervention and a meaningful probabilistic-accuracy endpoint remain undefined
   for this task family. The hypothesis is improvement, not a result to prove
   regardless of evidence. [Spec1-4; Step7; Step8]

2. Benefit, neutrality and harm, including estimator overhead -- UNMET.
   Width1 supplies a possible low-cost-variation control; width4/16 supply real
   variation. No policy/estimator has been tested, and there is no validated
   expensive-but-necessary workload group. These cases do not establish that a
   cost factor helps, does nothing, or harms. [Step6; Spec6,9]

3. Real PLN costs -- MET for the pinned runtime.
   Actual selection/inference/result/queue work is timed inside the engine; there
   are no sleeps or fabricated costs. Counts explain work but are not substituted
   for CPU. Four local profiling states remain unresolved. [Step5; Step6; Step8]

4. Meaningful query and independent probability target -- UNMET in full.
   The formal authorization query and its derivation target are explicit and
   independently replayed. There is no independently specified probabilistic
   world target for this family. Same-formula replay verifies PLN execution; it
   does not establish calibrated probability accuracy. Do not revive the old
   0.74 machine example as the oracle for this new task. [Spec1-4; Step7]

5. Distinguish probability accuracy from confidence -- MET as an evaluation rule.
   Confidence0.729 versus later approximately0.843262 is not scored as accuracy
   improvement. Deadline failure is not a false proposition. No probabilistic
   accuracy result is claimed. [Step3; Step7; Step8]

6. World mapping, evidence provenance, assumptions and stress cases -- UNMET in full.
   Formal axioms, unique evidence IDs, overlap guards and accepted MP/revision
   witnesses are explicit. A probabilistic-world mapping and balanced compatible/
   approximation-stress cases are absent. The formal proof-speed contract does
   not require inventing a hidden world. [Spec1-4; proof.py; Step3]

7. Speed, deadline success, probability error and continuation -- UNMET in full.
   Native first-goal CPU and budgeted success are measured. Step8 intentionally
   stops at the first acceptable goal; it does not produce an anytime error curve
   or evaluate probabilities after that point. Full derivation timing in Step7
   is not a substitute for those outputs. [Step7; Step8]

8. Strong matched factor-off/on baselines -- UNMET.
   Native confidence selection with a shared stopping adapter is available.
   No matched cost-aware arm exists; no relevance or simple structural-cost
   comparator was benchmarked on THIS task family. Earlier pilots on different
   tasks cannot establish which comparator is strongest here. [budget.py; Step8]

9. Independent rule/truth/evidence checks and rejection tests -- MET for the
   supported proof subset and acceptance metric. Actual certificates use pinned
   formula replay, licensed inputs, acyclic parents and disjoint evidence. Tests
   reject future-proof leakage, altered outputs, invalid timing and false/late
   success. This is not validation of every PLN rule or probabilistic semantics.
   [proof.py; tests/test_first_answer.py; tests/test_budgets.py; Step3-8]

10. Python-led real-PLN execution -- MET for the subprocess interface.
    Python orchestrates/tests/analyzes native MeTTa on pinned PeTTa/SWI. Small
    Prolog bridges read engine CPU and implement observation/stopping. No Python
    reasoner replaces PLN. Embedded MeTTa()/PeTTa compatibility is untested.
    [runtime.py; run_budgets.py; runtime.json]

11. Complete online costs and separate wall/memory/startup/training -- UNMET in full.
    The declared loaded-engine boundary charges selection and online goal/budget
    checks. Invocation wall and parent CPU are separate; they also include offline
    work and cannot be added blindly as answer latency. No estimator/training,
    peak-memory measurement or complete application-cost decomposition exists.
    Any future Python selector must have its online CPU charged. [Step7; Step8]

12. Pre-operation prediction without leakage; cost versus value -- UNRESOLVED.
    The existing online budget adapter receives no oracle checkpoint/proof and
    cannot rescue late goals offline. There is no cost predictor whose feature
    availability or labels can be audited. Next-operation CPU and delayed benefit
    remain separate prediction targets. [budget.py; budget_clock.pl; Step8]

13. Structural held-out evaluation and frozen estimator -- UNMET.
    All nine cases,13 saved states and630 budget runs are development evidence.
    Repetitions/orderings are not independent graph families. Instrumentation
    validation does not count as a held-out learning-validation set. This data
    alone does not establish that Bayesian policy search or DQN can be trained
    and generalized adequately. No model family is selected here. [Spec5; Step6-8]

14. Workload/budget calibration and explicit deadlines -- MET for the declared
    cooperative, loaded-engine deadline metric. The fixed grid spans failure,
    transition and saturation. Actual CPU/overshoot is retained. This is NOT
    strict equal CPU consumption or preemptive enforcement; redesign is needed
    if those become the requirement. Do not relabel the results as hard caps.
    [budget-protocol.json; Step7; Step8]

15. Repeats, randomization, retained failures and appropriate analysis -- MET
    for the local-cost comparison and descriptive budget sweep. Step6 uses
    process blocks, paired orders, intervals and corrected directional tests;
    four unresolved states remain. Step8 reports counts from ten repetitions,
    not confidence bounds for unseen tasks or a policy improvement estimate.
    Generalization inference still requires criterion13. [Step6; Step8]

16. Overhead/shuffled-cost controls and explanations of losses -- UNMET.
    Empty timing controls and online observation-window diagnostics exist, but
    there is no estimator-overhead-only arm, shuffled-cost arm or evaluation of
    cost-prediction mistakes/expensive-work starvation. [Step6; Step8]

17. Justified paper-to-PLN mapping -- UNRESOLVED for the final adaptation.
    Prior paper-discovery notes exist; they are not a completed mapping of a
    chosen method to this measured expansion/goal/budget interface. Revisit each
    primary paper's task, computations, utility, cost assumptions, data, baselines,
    experiments and limitations before choosing an adaptation. No paper guarantee
    or author-reported improvement is transferred to PLN by this audit.

18. Solo feasibility, bounded claims and review workflow -- MET for this phase.
    The completed experiments used the existing local CPU runtime, no GPU or new
    installation. Stepwise artifacts/failures were retained. This does not yet
    establish the feasibility of a larger learning-based research contribution.
    User review/commit ownership remains in force; no staging/commit this turn.

What is ready now
Use these tasks to debug and measure candidate cost-factor interventions after
paper/design review. Retain width1 controls, all orderings and unresolved local
profiles. The strongest local cost evidence is width16; that does not justify
silently removing other cases or treating width16 as held-out evaluation.
The existing failure/neutrality controls are preliminary measurement controls,
not completed demonstrations of policy benefit/neutrality/harm.

What must be resolved before the main comparison
A. Review the primary papers and define the exact intervention/observations and
   utility. Distinguish a cost penalty from a value-of-computation model.
B. Keep a common candidate set, detector, output rule and deadline accounting
   across arms; identify a cheap structural/relevance comparator and cost-only
   ablations. Changing the goal, pruning rules or adding indexing in one arm
   would confound the effect attributed to cost information.
C. Validate additional tasks where expensive work is necessary/useful, and where
   prediction has little value or costs more than it saves. Split structures
   before tuning. Repeated measurements of nine tiny KBs do not solve this.
D. Specify whether the study targets loaded-engine deadline success, actual
   resource consumption, full application latency, or more than one. Preserve
   overshoot or implement common finer/preemptible operations if necessary.
E. For the probabilistic track, define the target semantics/reference and shared
   output policy first. Exact agreement with pinned formulas is a legitimate
   implementation criterion, but not automatically external accuracy. A longer
   PLN run is a computational reference, not automatically probabilistic truth.
No one of these is being silently added as another preliminary run today. They
are the remaining design requirements for the research comparison.

Next reviewed step
Review Hay, Callaway/BMPS and the other already identified primary papers using
one common extraction sheet: problem/task; computation unit; observations and
state; benefit/utility; cost model; predictor inputs and labels; training burden;
selection/stopping mechanism; experimental baselines/budgets; results; limitations;
assumptions that do or do not hold for actual PLN expansions. Use primary sources,
not just the abstracts or our older notes. Compare cheap methods as seriously as
learned ones. Separate paper reproduction from a PLN adaptation. Stop for review
before selecting/implementing the method. No architecture or final proposal here.
