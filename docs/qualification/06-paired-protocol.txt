STEP 6 PROTOCOL, FIXED BEFORE CALIBRATION/MAIN MEASUREMENTS

Purpose: determine whether Ready A/B expansion CPU differs reproducibly on the
13 previously saved eligible states. No new KB selection, estimator or query
benchmark. Keep all three equal-width controls, successes and negative outcomes.
Read configs/paired-protocol.json for exact fixed numbers/seeds.

Operation: the previously validated native Cost.Expand, with no detailed logging.
Each iteration starts from the same original ordered Tasks/Beliefs, same pending
candidate and queue caps. Never feed the preceding expansion's queues into the
next. Immutable original inputs may be reused; hardware caches are not restored.
Check EVERY resulting queue/derivation tuple against the validated step-five
output, after stopping the timer. No unchecked last-result-only batching.

Harness: separate windows for preparing inputs and for a batch of K expansions.
The latter includes recursion/result-list construction and any engine GC during
the window. No automatic overhead subtraction. Matched empty batches return the
expected output without inference; they estimate framework/clock overhead, not
all possible observer effects. Detailed proof/counter logging remains disabled.
Startup, printing, correctness comparisons and parent Python remain outside the
local expansion window. They are not free in an eventual end-to-end benchmark.

Calibration only: three canonical initial states, N=1,4,16. Start with K=1;
if the 95th-percentile empty-batch CPU exceeds 10% of either candidate's median
batch CPU in ANY calibration state, try K=8 and then K=32. At each K, run all three
states in fresh processes, 20 warm-up pairs then 30 balanced randomized pairs and
30 empty batches. Pick the first qualifying K for every main condition. If none
qualifies, retain the failure and stop; do not collect interpretable main results.
Report early/late calibration medians as warm-up diagnostics, without adapting
warm-up after seeing a preferred A/B result. Calibration is excluded from the
main analysis. Fix the absolute practical-gap floor to five times the largest
calibration median empty CPU per expansion at the accepted K.

Main measurement: 12 blocks. Each block has one fresh process per eligible state;
randomize the 13 state orders in each block. Each process uses the same 20 warm-up
pairs, then 10 measured pairs (exactly five A-first, five B-first, shuffled) and
10 empty batches. Prewrite the whole schedule before starting. Same-process pairs
control some shared conditions; process blocks, not individual repetitions, are
the analysis units. Keep default GC and normal scheduling; no artificial delays,
forced GC or post-hoc exclusion of outliers. No sequential early stopping for a
favorable effect. Native failure or output mismatch invalidates the affected run.

Analysis, separately for every saved state:
- Per pair: batch CPU/K, B/A ratio and B-minus-A nanoseconds per expansion.
- Per process block: medians of those paired ratios and differences.
- Across 12 blocks: median effects, percentile bootstrap 95% intervals by resampling
  whole blocks (5000 draws, fixed seed), and a two-sided exact sign test on block
  differences. CIs are descriptive and conditional on these fixed tasks; they
  are not simultaneous intervals or generalization bounds over KB families.
- Require sign-test p <= .05/13 for a directional claim across the 13 states,
  ratio CI wholly beyond 1.10 (or below its reciprocal), absolute-difference CI
  beyond the calibrated floor, and matching practical direction in BOTH pair-order
  strata. The empty-batch p95 must also be <=10% of the faster median batch in
  EACH block; otherwise call instrumentation adequacy unresolved for that state.
- If the whole ratio CI fits within [1/1.10,1.10], report within that relative
  equivalence band (subject to instrument adequacy). Otherwise unresolved means
  inconclusive, not evidence of equal costs. State the absolute intervals too.
- Report the equal-width controls regardless of outcome. If any has a qualified
  practical directional gap, qualify any explanation attributing all wider-state
  differences to branch width; do not silently subtract or discard that control.

No universal cost-optimal selection, shorter proof time, better probabilities,
cross-component improvement or learned-estimator benefit follows from this test.
Stop after this step for user review. Do not proceed to budgeted query runs.
