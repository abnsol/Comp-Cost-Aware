STEP 6: MAIN PAIRED EXPANSION-COST RESULTS
Completed 2026-10-04. Stop for user review before query-level experiments.

Outcome
156 fresh engine processes completed, with 1560 measured A/B pairs (3120
expansions), plus fixed warm-ups and empty/clock samples. All timed outputs
matched their validated native result; pinned source/runtime provenance remained
unchanged. Execution passed; that does not mean every statistical gate passed.

8/13 states: B reproducibly costlier under every predeclared criterion.
1/13 states: within the predeclared relative equivalence band.
4/13 states: instrumentation adequacy unresolved, retaining all their data.

Method and scope
The main runner uses the SAME thin adapter, fixture generator, runtime invocation
layer and extracted native expansion as repair/calibrate/cal002. Contract checks
reject changed code, inputs, protocol or source/runtime provenance. Original
legacy calibration and all repair results remain preserved.
13 fixed development states x 12 fresh-process blocks x 10 paired repetitions.
Five A-first and five B-first pairs per process, randomized. Every call uses the
same original saved queues and queue caps. The complete state/pair schedule was
saved before measurement. No block was discarded, retried or replaced.
20 warm-up pairs, 30 clock probes and 10 empty samples per process.
The full solution set is checked before timing; every timed output is checked
after timing. Python orchestrates; actual PLN rule application, result collection
and native queue updates occur inside the embedded-engine CPU interval.

Unchanged criteria: 10% maximum empty-p95/faster-median proxy in EVERY block;
B/A ratio CI beyond 1.10 (or reciprocal); absolute gap CI beyond 11.915 us;
exact two-sided sign p <= .05/13; practical direction in both pair-order strata.
Bootstrap intervals use 5000 whole-block resamples. They are descriptive 95%
intervals for these fixed states, not simultaneous or task-generalization bounds.

Calibration passing did not guarantee every later block would pass. We do not
waive the gate because the wider-state ratios look convincing. Four states are
unresolved under the original contract. The N1 controls have near-one point
ratios, but only the swapped-facts control clears the full overhead criterion.
This is not evidence that every equal-width case has equivalent cost.

What this establishes
Actual equal-priority PLN expansion alternatives can have reliably different
CPU costs on this constructed workload, without artificial delays or cost labels.
All five N16 states passed: paired B/A medians 3.535-3.799, absolute gaps
554-631 us. Three of five N4 states passed: ratios 1.450-1.572, gaps 43-52 us.
All eight accepted differences have 12/12 positive blocks and exact sign
p=0.00048828125, below .05/13. Both pair-order strata pass the practical margin.
Prior separate work-counter validation observed one A result versus N B results
while both scan the same belief buffer. The CPU findings are consistent with
that additional generation/queue work. They do not isolate the exact fraction
of CPU due to matching, deduplication or queue management.

What this does not establish
- A cheaper first expansion need not give a cheaper complete proof. B also
  produces more intermediate results, which may have downstream value.
- The native selector has other equal-priority candidates, including rule
  records. The experiment forces A/B only to compare local costs; it does not
  demonstrate that freely running PLN would choose those alternatives next.
- There is no measured improvement to answer latency, probability accuracy,
  ECAN, a cost estimator or a cost-aware selection policy.
- Warm repeated ground-state expansions do not restore hardware caches or
  allocator history. Generalization across other machines, cold starts,
  symbolic tasks and probabilistic evidence remains untested.
- Startup, input preparation, selector work, verification and printing are
  outside this local window. Estimation and selection costs must count in a
  future end-to-end comparison. No overhead has been subtracted here.

Next proposed step (discuss before implementation)
Measure the unchanged native baseline to the first available goal whose proof
can be replayed, preserving all rule candidates and native queue transitions.
Locate when the measured alternatives occur relative to that event. Establish
a meaningful CPU-budget range before claiming that choosing A helps. Review
the baseline criteria and the papers before implementing any cost estimator.
Do not enlarge this local-cost result into a claim of faster or better answers.
