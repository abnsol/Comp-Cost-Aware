EXPERIMENT ACCEPTANCE CHECKLIST
Agreed research-wide criteria recorded 2026-10-04; reviewed after qualification
in docs/qualification/09-criteria-review.txt. That review marks all18 criteria
with evidence and scope. Preliminary measurement qualification is complete;
the full cost-factor/accuracy research benchmark is not yet ready.

The later approved first-valid-proof task contract narrows this preliminary
phase. Probability-accuracy and learned-method requirements below remain open;
they are not silently dropped or retroactively imposed on the proof-speed test.

For the baseline review, mark each criterion MET, UNMET or UNRESOLVED, with
evidence and scope. Design agreement alone does not establish empirical validity.

1. Precise question: incremental effect of cost information on speed and accuracy.
2. Cases allow benefit, no benefit and harm, including estimator overhead.
3. Real PLN costs; no artificial delays or unvalidated operation-count proxies.
4. Meaningful query and an independently established probability target.
5. Probability accuracy is distinct from PLN confidence.
6. Explicit world-to-PLN mapping, evidence provenance and inference assumptions;
   distinguish assumption-compatible and approximation-stress cases.
7. Measure time to a verified derivation, success by deadline and probability
   error at matched CPU budgets; continue measuring after the first answer.
8. Strong matched baselines; shared fixes and equivalent rules, inputs, queues,
   candidate generation and output selection across factor-off/on versions.
9. Independent checks of rules, truth calculations and evidence; fail closed on
   unsupported proof rules, and include rejection checks for altered traces.
10. Python-led execution with real PLN; verify the chosen runtime interface.
11. Charge all online Python/engine work and estimator overhead; separately
    report wall time, memory, startup, training and offline evaluation costs.
12. Cost prediction uses only information available before the operation;
    no free speculative execution or hidden future/test labels. Immediate cost
    prediction is not automatically delayed-benefit or value prediction.
13. Separate development, validation and structurally held-out evaluation;
    freeze estimator and settings before test access.
14. Calibrated workload sizes and budgets, with explicit deadline handling.
15. Reproducible paired comparisons, timing repeats, randomized execution order,
    retained failures and uncertainty estimates without pseudoreplication.
16. Overhead-only and shuffled-cost controls where appropriate; explain gains
    and losses, including expensive-path starvation and prediction errors.
17. Justified paper-to-PLN mapping; separate reproductions from adaptations and
    avoid transferring guarantees whose assumptions do not hold.
18. Solo-researcher feasibility, bounded claims and stepwise review/commits.
